\section*{Research Summary}

**Kronos vs AlphaZeroBeta: a controlled, leakage-free comparison of a financial\par
foundation model and a reinforcement-learning portfolio agent.**\par

Market: S\&P 500. Out-of-sample: 22 non-overlapping 6-month walk-forward folds,\par
2014-01 .. 2024-12. Identical universe, costs and constraints for every model.\par
All numbers below are generated from the run artefacts in \texttt{results/}; nothing is\par
typed by hand.\par

\subsection*{1. Literature review}

Four literatures bear on this study.\par

*Financial time-series foundation models.* General time-series foundation models\par
showed that large-scale pre-training transfers across domains. Financial\par
foundation models specialise the idea to price bars, whose heavy tails and near\par
zero signal-to-noise ratio differ from other series. Kronos is the open-weights\par
example used here.\par

*Deep RL for portfolio management.* Policy-gradient methods were applied to\par
allocation early; later work added deep actors, distributional value estimates\par
and simulated market environments. The recurring design problem is the reward:\par
pure return maximisation produces concentrated, high-turnover books, so\par
practical systems penalise volatility, benchmark correlation and turnover.\par

*Market-neutral and statistical-arbitrage construction.* Dollar neutrality and\par
gross-exposure caps are the standard constraints, and beta neutrality is usually\par
enforced by projection rather than by optimising a beta penalty alone.\par

*Backtest methodology.* The central hazards are look-ahead bias, survivorship\par
bias, and overfitting through repeated specification search. Walk-forward\par
evaluation is the standard mitigation; block bootstrap, Diebold-Mariano and\par
Newey-West are the standard inference tools for autocorrelated, heteroskedastic\par
series. This study adopts those tools rather than inventing new ones.\par

\subsection*{2. Research gap}

Forecast-first and decision-first systems are scored with incompatible\par
metrics: error metrics on a forecasting task for one, risk-adjusted portfolio\par
return in a backtest for the other. Consequently, published headline numbers\par
across the two literatures are not comparable, and no controlled head-to-head\par
comparison of a financial foundation model against an RL portfolio agent under\par
a single protocol was found. This study builds that protocol and reports what it\par
measures.\par

\subsection*{3. Dataset}

Public daily bars for the S\&P 500, adjusted for splits and dividends with a\par
same-day factor. 476 tickers x 2924 trading days (2013-06-03 .. 2025-01-14).\par
Investable set: the top 100 names by trailing 60-day mean dollar volume,\par
recomputed per date and applied identically to every arm. Known bias: current\par
index membership (survivorship), stated rather than hidden.\par

\subsection*{9. Statistical honesty check}

1. Does Kronos outperform AlphaZeroBeta? Computed from the artefacts, not\par
   asserted: see the Sharpe differences in 8.3.\par
2. On which metrics? Reported per metric in 8.2.\par
3. On which markets? One (S\&P 500). Scope decision, stated as a limitation.\par
4. Statistically significant? Judged solely by whether the block-bootstrap\par
   interval excludes zero in 8.3.\par
5. Survives transaction costs? See 8.5.\par
6. Survives regimes? See 8.4.\par
7. Survives multiple seeds? See 8.6, which reports the worst seed.\par
8. Survives parameter changes? Partially: the primary sensitivity is cost;\par
   architecture-level sensitivity was not run and is listed as a limitation.\par
9. Better at forecasting but worse at portfolio construction? Level 1 and\par
   Level 2 are reported separately precisely so this can be read off directly.\par
10. Caused by different objectives? Discussed in the paper's Discussion; the\par
    two systems optimise different objectives, so the comparison measures\par
    outcomes under a shared protocol, not the merit of either objective.\par

Experimental runs logged: 31.\par

\subsection*{11. Limitations}

- One market (S\&P 500), one decade, daily frequency, one currency.\par
- Current index membership: survivorship bias present and not removable with\par
  public data.\par
- Price and volume features only; no fundamentals, analyst revisions or\par
  sentiment, unlike the source study's vendor panel.\par
- The RL arm trains under a reduced compute budget and with one seed on the full\par
  fold set; three seeds on a subset.\par
- Kronos is evaluated zero-shot only; fine-tuning was out of scope.\par
- The RL encoder treats assets independently rather than flattening all assets\par
  into one joint observation.\par
- Cost modelling is a fixed per-side schedule plus a flat borrow rate; no\par
  market-impact model.\par
- No forecasting claim is made about AlphaZeroBeta, because it emits no\par
  forecast and manufacturing one would be a fabrication.\par

\subsection*{12. Final conclusion}

This study does not identify a universally dominant method. The market-neutral\par
arms are compared on risk-adjusted return with interval estimates; the\par
forecasting arm is compared against a random walk; and the reproduction gap on\par
AlphaZeroBeta is reported rather than adjusted away. Claims are made only where\par
the statistics support them, and every deviation from the source methodologies\par
is disclosed in \texttt{docs/reproduction.md}.\par
